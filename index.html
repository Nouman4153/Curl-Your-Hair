Laifen AutoCurl Curling Iron: A Smarter Way to Curl Your Hair

Automatic curling irons are supposed to save time, but some still leave you doing more work than expected. You may need to position the hair carefully, choose a rotation direction for every section, or stop midway because a strand has caught inside the chamber.

The newly launched Laifen AutoCurl takes a more automated approach. It detects the hair, draws it into the curling chamber, wraps it around a 32 mm barrel, and manages the curling direction for you.

For anyone looking for an automatic curling iron for easy styling, that hands-off wrapping system is the main attraction. But the AutoCurl also brings together temperature control, negative ions, anti-tangle protection, dual-voltage support, and a lightweight body that weighs 388 grams.

AutoCurl Does More of the Work for You

The biggest difference between the AutoCurl and a traditional curling wand is obvious the moment you use it. With a regular wand, you take a section of hair, wrap it around the heated barrel by hand, hold it in place, and then carefully release it. The result can look great, but the process depends heavily on your technique.

The AutoCurl removes much of that manual wrapping.

Place a section of hair into the open chamber and the system detects it automatically. Laifen says its sensing system can recognize hair placement in about 0.5 seconds before starting the wrapping process.

The tool also detects curling direction. Instead of repeatedly choosing left or right rotation as you move around your head, the dual-chamber sensing system is designed to recognize placement and alternate the direction automatically.

The barrel itself measures 32 mm, or about 1.25 inches, which makes it better suited to loose curls, soft waves, and fuller everyday styles rather than tiny, tightly wound curls.

The chamber is enclosed rather than leaving the full heated barrel exposed. That makes the styling process feel quite different from working with a conventional wand, especially around the back of the head where seeing exactly where your fingers are can be difficult.

The idea is simple: you still choose the section and position the tool, but the AutoCurl handles the repetitive twisting.

Hair Detection, Heat Control, and Tangle Protection Work Together

The automatic wrapping gets most of the attention, but there's quite a lot happening inside the tool once the hair enters the chamber.

The AutoCurl uses dual ceramic PTC heating elements and reaches styling temperature in roughly 50 seconds. You can choose between three temperature settings: 160°C, 180°C, and 200°C.

That range lets you adjust the heat instead of using the same temperature on every hair type.

Laifen also uses PID temperature control that checks temperature around 1,000 times per second. The point isn't simply to heat the barrel quickly. It's to keep the temperature stable while you're styling instead of allowing large heat fluctuations. The official product information also lists a 40W rated power and 100–240V input.

Then there's the ionic system.

The AutoCurl releases up to 200 million negative ions per cubic centimeter during styling. Laifen positions this as a way to reduce static, smooth the hair surface, and leave the finished curl looking shinier rather than dry or fluffy.

The anti-tangle system may be even more important for an automatic curler.

The motor monitors resistance while hair is being drawn around the barrel. If it detects unusual pulling or a possible tangle, the system is designed to stop and reverse instead of continuing to tighten the strand.

That's the sort of feature you may not notice when everything is going well, but it's exactly the feature you want when something doesn't.

Using the Laifen AutoCurl Is Pretty Simple

The AutoCurl is designed around a much easier routine than manually wrapping every strand around a hot barrel. That doesn't mean you should grab huge sections of hair and expect perfect curls immediately, but the actual process is straightforward.

Start with dry, detangled hair and split it into manageable sections.

Step 1: Place the Hair

Take roughly a one-inch section and place it into the opening of the curling chamber.

Laifen recommends the AutoCurl for hair lengths of approximately 25 to 60 cm, or around 9.8 to 23.6 inches.

Smaller, consistent sections will usually give the automatic wrapping system an easier job and help the curls look more even.

Step 2: Let It Wrap

Once the sensor recognizes the strand, the AutoCurl draws the hair in and wraps it around the 32 mm barrel automatically.

You can choose between the three available temperatures depending on your hair and how much heat you want to use.

The automatic mode is meant to handle most of the timing and direction for you, so there's no need to twist your wrist around the barrel.

Step 3: Release the Curl

Once the curl is set, pull the tool downward and let the hair slide out of the chamber.

From there, you can leave the curl intact for more definition or let it cool and then loosen it with your fingers for a softer wave.

The AutoCurl also offers manual control when you want more say over rotation.

That's useful because the product isn't locking you into one completely automated style. You can use the automatic system when convenience matters and take more control when you're trying to create a specific look.

The Dyson Airwrap and Shark FlexStyle have a clear advantage if you want one system that can dry, smooth, volumize, and curl using different attachments.

The AutoCurl isn't trying to compete with that versatility.

Its advantage is simplicity. There are no curling barrels to swap, no airflow technique to learn, and no need to manually wrap the hair.

It's Built to Be Easy to Travel With Too

Once a hair tool leaves the bathroom, details like weight and voltage suddenly become much more important.

The AutoCurl weighs 388 grams without the cord. That's light enough that using it through a full styling session should feel more manageable than some larger automatic stylers, especially when you're working on the back sections of your hair.

The body also uses an enclosed anti-scald design. Instead of leaving the hot barrel fully exposed, the curling chamber helps keep your fingers and skin farther away from the heated surface.

The AutoCurl supports 100–240V dual voltage, which is particularly useful if you travel internationally. You may still need the correct plug adapter for the country you're visiting, but you don't need a separate voltage converter in supported regions.

Safety features continue beyond the enclosed chamber.

The tool includes an automatic sleep function and auto shut-off protection, so forgetting to switch it off immediately is less stressful than it would be with a basic curling wand.

The official product listing also states an operating noise level of no more than 45 dB and confirms a two-year warranty for the AutoCurl.

The package includes the AutoCurl itself, two styling clips, safety information, a quick-start guide, and warranty documentation.

These aren't glamorous features, but they make a difference if the curling iron is something you plan to use regularly rather than only for special occasions.

Who Is the Laifen AutoCurl Best For?

The AutoCurl makes the most sense for someone who likes the look of loose curls and waves but doesn't love the process of creating them with a traditional wand.

Beginners are an obvious fit.

Wrapping the back sections of your own hair around a hot barrel while looking into a mirror takes more coordination than it seems. The automatic hair detection and wrapping system takes much of that repetitive movement out of your hands.

It's also a good fit for people who already know how to curl their hair but want to make the process quicker and less tiring.

The 32 mm barrel naturally suits larger curls and relaxed waves, so someone who mostly wants very tight curls may be better served by a smaller barrel.

Hair length matters too. Laifen recommends the tool for hair roughly 25 to 60 cm long, so extremely short hair may not give the automatic chamber enough length to work with effectively.

The other audience is frequent travelers. The lighter 388 g body and 100–240V dual-voltage design make the AutoCurl easier to take abroad than a large multi-styler system.

And because Laifen built this as a dedicated curling tool rather than a dryer-and-styler combination, the experience stays focused. You aren't paying for a collection of attachments if what you mainly want is an easier way to curl your hair.

That narrower focus is actually one of the product's strengths.

Conclusion

The Laifen AutoCurl isn't trying to make curling more complicated. It's trying to remove the parts people often find annoying.

You don't manually wrap every strand. You don't have to constantly decide which direction the barrel should rotate. And if the system detects unusual resistance, it is designed to stop and reverse instead of continuing to pull.

Around that automatic curling system, Laifen has added a 32 mm ceramic barrel, three temperature settings, PID temperature monitoring, up to 200 million negative ions, an enclosed anti-scald chamber, and a lightweight 388 g body. Official Laifen information also confirms the 100–240V dual-voltage design and two-year warranty.

Compared with a traditional curling wand, the AutoCurl requires far less manual wrapping. Compared with tools like the Dyson Airwrap or Shark FlexStyle, it's less versatile, but also more focused and straightforward.

That's really the reason to consider it.

If you want one tool dedicated to creating loose curls and waves—and you'd rather let the tool handle the wrapping—the AutoCurl offers a much simpler way to get there.
